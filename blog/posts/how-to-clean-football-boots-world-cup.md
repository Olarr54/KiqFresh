The World Cup does something to people. Suddenly everyone's digging their boots out from the back of the wardrobe, booking five-a-sides, and watching three games a day. Which means millions of football boots are about to take a serious beating.

Here's how to look after them properly.

## Why boot cleaning actually matters

Mud, grass and sweat left on leather and synthetic uppers breaks down the material over time. Studs and outsoles accumulate dirt that affects your grip. And if you're buying decent boots — which most people are — it's worth spending ten minutes after a session to protect the investment.

## What you need

* A firm brush (a dedicated boot brush or an old toothbrush)
* A soft cloth or sponge
* Warm water
* Mild soap or specialist boot cleaner
* Newspaper or old socks for stuffing

## Step 1: Remove the laces and insoles

Always start here. Laces trap dirt and insoles hold moisture — both need separate treatment. Soak the laces in warm soapy water. Leave the insoles out to air dry separately.

## Step 2: Knock off the big stuff first

Tap the soles together or use your brush to remove loose mud while it's still slightly damp. Dried mud is much harder to shift. Do this right after your session, not two days later.

## Step 3: Clean the outsole and studs

Scrub the studs and outsole firmly with your brush and warm water. A toothbrush gets into the stud grooves properly. Check the studs are tight while you're at it — loose studs are a common cause of boot damage.

## Step 4: Clean the upper — carefully

This is where material matters.

**Leather boots** (Copa Mundial, Tiempo, etc): use a small amount of leather cleaner on a soft cloth. Wipe in one direction, don't scrub. Follow up with leather conditioner once dry — leather that dries out cracks.

**Synthetic and knit uppers** (most modern boots): warm water and a soft cloth. Gentle circular motion. Don't soak them — water getting into the sole bond weakens it over time.

**Suede panels**: dry brush only. Water stains suede permanently. If they're badly marked, take them to a professional.

## Step 5: Dry properly

This is where most people go wrong. Never put boots near a radiator or in direct sunlight — heat warps the shape and cracks synthetic materials. Stuff them with newspaper or old socks to hold their shape and leave them somewhere with airflow overnight.

## Step 6: Protect them

Once dry, use a waterproof spray on synthetic boots and a conditioner on leather. This massively extends their lifespan and makes them easier to clean next time.

## When home cleaning isn't enough

Some things you can't fix at home. Yellowed soles, deep set stains, scuffed leather panels, mould from being stored damp — these need professional attention.
